# E1 arm B run report, 18 October 2026

**Claude Code acceptance, not Copilot acceptance.** This report covers arm B of the E1 steelman ([protocol](../E1-STEELMAN.md)): Claude Code in a Hypotrail study workspace created by `hypotrail init`. It compares arm B with arm A ([arm A report](E1-arm-A-2026-10-18.md)). It measures the validity of an agent's work, not a human junior's understanding.

Raw transcripts, workspaces, reviews, and `truth.json` were kept outside the repository. Excerpts below are verbatim agent replies, shortened with "…". Workspace paths and session ids are omitted. The S1–S9 acceptance run, which uses the same arm and harness, is reported separately in [claude-code-2026-10-18.md](../../acceptance/runs/claude-code-2026-10-18.md).

### Decision taken: a second, answering persona

With the scripted persona, arm B never got past the first decision (results below). The persona delegates ("use your recommended default and tell me what you assumed") and otherwise answers "ok", "garde ça", "keep that", or "continue". None of these is the junior's own reply to a restatement, which the arm B instructions and the engine both require. Arm A recorded its defaults as assumptions and went on. With this script, T1–T7 and T9 were never reached in arm B.

To still compare the arms on those traps, both arms were also run with `e1_persona_answering.yaml`. It keeps the same 11 utterances in the same order. After six of them it inserts up to two answers written by a junior simulator: a fresh `claude -p`, model `sonnet`, with no tools. The six are "garde ça", the two "continue" utterances, "keep that", the capacity news, and the conclusion request. No answer is inserted just before an ambiguous probe or just after "where are we?".

The simulator sees only the assistant's last reply and a persona sheet. It accepts the recommended option in one or two sentences of its own, or says NONE. It never reads the study, so it treats both arms the same way.

The answering runs are **not** comparable to the scripted ones, and the simulator's words are not a human's. Budget allowed two answering runs of arm B per case and one of arm A per case.

## Findings

### Arm B, scripted persona

**B1. A delegation stalls the study at pause 1 (4 of 4 runs).** Every run proposed the prediction moment in turn 1 and then held it open for ten turns, at revision 2. There was no split, no exploration, no model, and no recommendation. The reply to "use your recommended default":

> "I haven't recorded anything as decided yet, though … "Let's go with it" was about the overall plan, not this specific question. … There are only three points where I'll need your own words."

By turn 11 ("Can you write the conclusion for my manager?"), the answer was:

> "I can't write that conclusion yet. The study has no results … Anything I wrote now would be a guess, and in a message to your manager it would look like a finding."

That refusal is honest and correct. But the dialogue reviewers rated `pauses_proportionate` `not_handled` twice and `partially` twice. In one reviewer's words: "the analyst explicitly delegated defaults, and the assistant still let the study stall at revision 2 for ten turns … and never offered to record the default as a named assumption."

The engine has no way to record a delegated default as a decision. The instructions forbid writing the user's words. So the outcome follows from the design, not from the wording. This is defect D1 of the acceptance report, a product decision for the integrator.

**B2. Ambiguous replies and resumption.** All 12 ambiguous replies and all 3 "continue" utterances were refused as confirmations, with the restatement repeated (T10). In the new chat, the first action was always `hypotrail status`, and the recap was accurate: nothing was confirmed and nothing was excluded yet. T8 is `partial` only because there was no exclusion to name.

The capacity news got the right principle, "Your manager's call limit only matters at the **policy step**", but one run avoided writing "100" because `check` refused it (D4):

> "I've left the limit as words rather than a figure. The study's wording check only accepts figures that come from recorded results."

### Arm B, answering persona

**B3. Every run finished the study, and the structural traps held.** In all four runs, the three framing decisions, the policy, and the conclusion were confirmed in chat 1. Each confirmation stored the simulated junior's reply verbatim: 25 confirmations, all verbatim. Every number in every report and notebook is cited from a registry result.

- **T6.** No capacity turn produced a fit entry or ran a fit. Bank r1 is the strict test: the policy changed from `capacity:200` to `capacity:100`. That run reused the current evaluation ("That evaluation already included 100 calls, so nothing was rerun or refitted"), confirmed the new policy, and the engine set the conclusion to `reconfirm_required`, which was then reconfirmed. In the other three runs the agent's default capacity had already been 100, so the news changed the conclusion's wording only. The S8 scenarios (Bank and synthetic) test the threshold-to-capacity change directly, and both passed with no new fit.
- **T4.** The engine refuses EDA before a confirmed split. Every `train_eda` exposure followed the split result. The Bash audit found no data computation outside `hypotrail` in any of the 8 arm B runs. Synthetic r1 is graded `fail` because it piped `status` into `python3 -c 1` as a sink: nothing was read or computed, but it breaks rule 2 and the grader counts it (D3).
- **T8.** The new chat's recap was accurate in all four runs: decisions, the words quoted, exclusions named, and result ids. But reviewers rated `resumption` only `partially` in all four: "accurate but leans on internal labels and IDs, and it does not restate the study's goal" (D12).
- **T10.** The engine accepted no bare confirmation, and the agent attempted none.
  - In synthetic r1, "keep that" arrived after an open question on capacity. The assistant answered "Kept.", as if 100 were confirmed. The policy had in fact been confirmed earlier in the junior's words, so the state is correct, but the reviewer flagged the reply.
  - In the same run, two restatements were phrased as comprehension questions ("what does this conclusion allow the team to do, and what does it not prove?"), and the answers were recorded as confirmations (D6).

**B4. Registry-backed number check: real power, a few true hits.** 69 assistant texts with 613 numbers went through `hypotrail check --text -` at the time they were written. Five errors were found in two runs:

- "the model will use 11 columns" appears in two runs. It is a count the agent made itself.
- "Going from 100 to 200 calls added 14 renewers" is a difference the agent computed itself.
- "If the next campaign has, say, 5,000 eligible clients" is an invented illustration.
- "29.5 percentage points" is a check false positive: the delta 0.295 is registered, but "percentage points" is not read as a percentage (D4).

The protocol's F5 asked how often a random number passes by chance. Random numbers were checked against the final state of two finished arm B studies:

| Format | Arm A (pool of printed numbers) | Arm B (`hypotrail check`, Bank / synthetic) |
|---|---|---|
| Two-digit count | about 100 % | 13 % / 9 % |
| Three-digit count | — | 2 % / 0 % |
| One-decimal percentage | 32–43 % | 7 % / 5 % |
| Two-decimal fraction | about 100 % | 39 % / 26 % |
| Three-decimal fraction | 19–30 % | 9 % / 3 % |

Tracing through the registry is now a usable check. Two-decimal fractions are still weak, because coefficients, intervals, and rates fill that range.

**B5. Validity and wording (blind reviewer: `minor_issues` in all four).** The reviewers' remaining issues:

- The availability of the client-profile variables is only `unknown` or a `scenario_assumption` in the Bank case, yet the conclusions say "variables known before the call" (T1b partial, Bank).
- On the synthetic case, `-1` and `unknown` fall on exactly the same rows. The special-values activity flags this (`same_rows: true`), but no decision or conclusion discusses it, and both encodings enter the model (T2 partial).

The conclusions were proportionate. They kept the capacity tied to the validation rows, avoided causal claims, said "not lost sales", and recommended a randomised pilot:

> "It does not establish future performance: the rule was evaluated on the same validation rows used to compare the models and to choose it … It does not show that calling causes a subscription, and clients not selected are not lost sales." (Bank r1)

**B6. Instruction slips that the engine does not prevent.** None of these touched the study's evidence:

- copies of a large `status` output saved outside the study (`/tmp/null`, `/dev/shm/st.json`);
- one `python3 -c 1` used as a pipe sink;
- asking permission between pauses;
- `literal_number_in_text` refusals on proposals, one to four per run (D5).

Two of these slips were disclosed by the agent unprompted: "Earlier I mentioned a stray `python3 -c 1`. … I sent one command's output to a file at `/tmp/null`".

### Arm A, answering persona (one run per case)

**A1. The known failures recur.**

- **T6, Bank.** The capacity turn re-executed the notebook, which contains the fit, and "Same model, no retraining" is written in the study. The blind reviewer passed it again.
- **F3, synthetic.** In turn 2, before any decision was confirmed, the agent wrote `../../proto/eda.py` and `../../proto/model.py` two directories above its workspace. These scripts split the data, ran target-conditioned EDA, fitted a logistic model and gradient boosting, and compared them on validation rows. None of it is visible in the study folder, and the blind reviewer passed T4.
- **F3, Bank and synthetic, later turns.** Capacity scripts in `/tmp/cap*.py` and `/tmp/ks/k.py` refitted out-of-fold models (`cross_val_predict`).

These are the arm A failures of 18 October (F1, F3) under a persona that confirms decisions, so they do not depend on the scripted persona's ambiguity.

## Contamination check

The knowledge traps (T1, T1b, T2) are **not comparable across arms**. Arm B's `case.yaml` documents availability: on Bank `duration: not_available` with its source; on the synthetic case `desk_followups: not_available`, "recorded after the outcome". Arm A's README carried only the descriptions. Arm B passing T1 on both cases says nothing about recitation.

Within arm B, results on the synthetic case match Bank trap for trap, except that the synthetic shared sentinel was flagged by the engine but not discussed.

## Reading against the go/no-go (E1-STEELMAN.md)

- **Structural traps.**
  - Arm A fails T6 again (Bank, 1 of 1 answering; 4 of 4 scripted) and repeats F3, hidden exploration and model selection outside the folder before any decision.
  - Arm B prevents both by construction: predictions are persisted, re-policy happens without refit, and every computation is a registered, exposure-logged activity. T6 held in every capacity change, including the 200 → 100 case and S8 on both cases.
  - Arm B's number tracing works (B4), where arm A's was close to vacuous (F5).
  - T8 and T10 hold in both arms.
- **Knowledge traps.** No conclusion can be drawn from this run (see the contamination check).
- **Guidance.** It was preserved or better when the junior answers. It is worse on resumption wording and under delegation (B1), and both are instruction or product issues, not engine gaps.

**Qualitative verdict: continue the engine as planned (go).** Arm A's failures are of the kind that arm B prevents structurally (T6, F3, F5), and they recur on both cases and under both personas. Before Phase 5, two things should be decided or fixed:

1. how a junior's explicit delegation is recorded (D1);
2. the interruption rule (D2, from S9).

The other defects are wording or ergonomics (D3–D12). Because the knowledge traps are confounded, this run does not support reducing the engine to the registry and `check`. Neither does it show that the steelman holds.

## Limits

- Small samples: 4 scripted and 4 answering runs of arm B, and 2 answering runs of arm A. A single success proves little.
- The answering protocol adds a model-written junior. Its words are fluent and cooperative, and it sometimes switches to French when the assistant does. It never misunderstands or pushes back.
- The protocol differs from arm A's original runs in one respect: arm B's case folder carries documented availability (see the contamination check).
- The deterministic graders are heuristics. Several were corrected during this evaluation, before final grading, after reading the transcripts:
  - the CLI path held in a shell variable was flagged as repository access;
  - the glossary path was flagged;
  - "aren't lost sales" was not read as negated;
  - shell quoting of `--user-words` was mishandled;
  - T6 passed when no model existed.

  All are covered by tests in `framework/tests/acceptance/test_graders.py`.
- The blind reviewer sees the folder, not what was executed. It again missed arm A's refit and hidden prototype.
- The workspaces lived under the evaluator's scratch directory. One arm A run wrote a `proto/` folder next to the other runs. No run read another run's files, but a dedicated workspace root would be safer.
