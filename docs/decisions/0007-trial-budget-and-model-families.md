## Context

### Review-v1 amendments (implemented 22 October 2026)

J1 replaces complement-of-block temporal fitting and shuffled inner tuning with explicit
supported memberships. J2 enforces claim/diagnostic requirements and distinguishes planned
challengers from diagnostic-informed choices. J3 aligns tuning and selection with the
declared action metric and separates point-estimate success from qualified lower-bound
success. See `docs/plans/reliability-roadmap/` and the acceptance records. The legacy
algorithms and three-variant budget remain unchanged. Independence of an outer score still
does not establish future-scenario validity when training includes later observations.

v0.2b allowed exactly one variant of the candidate model, a hand-picked weaker or stronger penalty or the model without one group of variables, and only the linear family. The aim was to limit forking paths before any independent evidence existed. Now that a sealed final test is read once at the end, several development trials are acceptable, provided they are counted, compared on the same folds, and chosen by a stated rule. In the second demo the chosen variant was a sensitivity check, not a search for a better model, and the penalty was never tuned (R24). The owner validated three things (R24bis). The first is a small trial budget, each variant declared with its question. The second is hyperparameters chosen by nested cross-validation, one tuning being one trial. The third is a model family chosen from a catalogue that includes an interpretable non-linear family, the Explainable Boosting Machine. A non-linear family is proposed only when the diagnostics of the linear fit show something it cannot follow. R28 adds that the diagnostics must run without the agent remembering to run them.

## Decision

1. **Up to three variants, each with its question.** The `model_variant` value becomes `{"variants": [...]}`: one to three variants (`trials.MAX_VARIANTS`), each `{kind, ..., question}`. The kinds are `regularisation` (weak or strong, as in v0.2b), `drop_group`, `tuned` (the candidate's penalty chosen by nested cross-validation), and `family` (`ebm` or `hgb`). Variant *i* is fitted into the slot `fit.variant` (*i* = 1, the v0.2b slot, so earlier identities are unchanged), `fit.variant:2` or `fit.variant:3`. Its registry entry keeps `activity: "fit.variant"` and `params.variant_index`. The question is not part of the model's identity, so a reworded question refits nothing.
2. **The budget counts what was confirmed.** Every distinct variant model the study ever confirmed counts (`trials.used_specs`, read from the confirmed proposals), whatever the current list holds. A value that would bring it above three is refused (`invalid_value`, `details.budget`). Replacing a variant therefore spends a trial. A v0.2b value (one variant, no question) is still accepted and is read as a list of one: an existing study has used one variant of its budget. Nothing in the state is rewritten.
3. **Nested cross-validation.** A `tuned` or `family` variant carries a grid in `params.tuning` (`trials.GRIDS`, simplest value first). These are the logistic `C` over 0.01 to 100, the ridge `alpha` over 100 to 0.01, the EBM's number of pairwise interactions (0 or 3), and the boosting depth (2 or 4). Legacy studies retain their historical seeded inner folds and ROC AUC/MAE choice. In `review-v1`, every inner plan is a subset of its outer training membership, preserves the declared temporal/group constraints, and picks by the predeclared precision/recall or row-weighted MAE objective. No infeasible plan falls back to shuffle. The model is then refitted on the outer training rows with that value and scores the outer fold. Preparation is learnt inside each inner fold. The values record the inner plan/digest and chosen value per fold and on all eligible development training rows. The final-test refit chooses before any final-test row is read.
4. **The catalogue.** `linear` is the default and comes first: logistic regression, or linear regression with ridge for the `tuned` variant. `ebm` is the Explainable Boosting Machine of `interpret-core` (MIT licence, pinned `0.7.3` in `framework/pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`), with one outer bag, no inner bag, at most 500 rounds at learning rate 0.05, `n_jobs=1`, and the split's seed. `hgb` is scikit-learn's histogram gradient boosting with 100 iterations, no early stopping, and categories ordinal-encoded as native categories. It is fitted with one OpenMP thread, so the result does not depend on the machine's thread count. The module sources of `interpret._version` and `sklearn` are part of the code fingerprint of `fit.variant` and `final_test.evaluation`, so another library version makes those results stale. The fit values record `versions`. Each family brings its own diagnostics as registered results. The EBM gives a shape function per feature and its interaction terms, by importance, from the fit on every development row. The boosting model gives the permutation importance of each input column on the rows each fold's model did not see. The linear models keep their coefficients and bootstrap intervals.
5. **The shortlist is drafted by the engine.** `view.model_families` reads two things. The first is the case's optional `constraints.interpretability` (`required`, `preferred`, `none`; absent means `none`); `required` excludes the black box. The second is `linear_checks`, three checks of the candidate's out-of-fold predictions that the development diagnostics now record. Non-linearity is the binned gap between observed and predicted per numeric feature, with a chi-square test and a Bonferroni correction. An interaction is a calibration slope that differs by segment (Cochran's Q). The overall calibration slope (Cox 1958) is flagged outside [0.8, 1.2] when its interval excludes 1. Without a flag the recommendation is to stay linear. With a flag the glass box (EBM) is listed first, then boosting when the case allows it. A `family` variant proposed while a flag stands is reported `data_contingent` by `decide propose`, with the flags as the reason.
6. **The selection rule.** Each evaluation that compares variants stores `model_selection` (`trials.select`), so every number is registered. Legacy studies retain the per-fold ROC AUC/MAE, one-standard-error and descriptive optimism reading. In `review-v1`, the winner is the pooled-count precision/recall at the fixed rule or row-weighted MAE declared by the criterion. Pooled-count values are not equal-weight fold means, so the legacy one-SE and optimism outputs are explicitly unavailable; reports and notebooks say why. The trial budget remains a disclosure, not a statistical correction.
7. **Diagnostics are part of the plan (R28).** The engine does not chain a diagnostics run behind a fit. `next_action` makes it the mandatory next step instead. While no variant is named, the `models` step lists `diag.binary` or `diag.regression` after the fits. These are the diagnostics of the first linear fit, which the shortlist reads. The choice step (`policy` or `model_choice`) lists it after the evaluation. A confirmed variant decision that names other variants makes the current diagnostics stale, so they are run again once the variants are fitted. Adopting a rule other than the one they were computed at makes them stale too (WP-A, R34), so the confusion matrix at the adopted rule is always computed before the plan moves on. Before a variant is named, the diagnostics need only the candidate and the baseline; once named, every variant fit must be current.
8. **The final test is unchanged in principle.** One configuration is chosen before the opening: a model slot (the candidate or one variant, so one family) and, for a binary study, a rule (`capacity:100@fit.variant:2`). The opening refuses while a declared variant's fit or the evaluation is stale; a variant no longer named does not block it. Any variant named or fitted after the opening is "after the final test" (`partition.AFTER_DECISIONS`, `AFTER_SLOT_PREFIXES`).

## Consequences

- A study may fit up to four model configurations (the candidate and three variants), each counted. The report states the budget, the rule that picked one, and its optimism.
- Fits are slower. On a few hundred rows, a tuned linear variant takes seconds, the boosting variant about fifteen seconds, and the EBM about thirty (forty-odd small fits for nested cross-validation). On Bank full or Hotel they take minutes. The grids and fixed settings are small on purpose.
- The inner folds are shuffled k-fold even under temporal outer folds. Identical records grouped by the case's `duplicates` policy may fall in different inner folds. Both affect only the choice of the hyperparameter, never the out-of-fold figures, and are stated in CLI-CONTRACT §14.
- Existing results stay current. The engine change touches fingerprinted modules (`activities`, `fit`, `governance`, `diagnostics`), which would have made every fit, evaluation and diagnostic of existing studies "code changed", including the one evaluation of an opened final test that can never run again. Every configuration a study could hold before computes the same values, so the pre-WP-F1 digests of those four modules are declared equivalent (`mlkit.study.code_compat`). Earlier results only lack the new additive keys, and the shortlist asks to run the diagnostics again for the linear checks. A v0.2b study keeps its variant decision and the identity of its first variant. Its plan now asks for the diagnostics when they are missing.
- The one-variant rule of the WP-B1 amendment is replaced. Everything else in ADR 0006 and its amendments stands.
